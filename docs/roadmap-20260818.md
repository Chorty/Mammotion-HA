# Session findings and a roadmap — 2026-08-18

Two parts: what this session established (with pointers to the detail), and
where the integration should go next. The roadmap is built on the operator's
own standing decisions — **this yard only**, **night parked**, **accuracy
closed**, and the goal being **consistency, not precision: click-to-go reliable
enough to trust without watching**.

---

# Part 1 — What this session established

## Shipped and accepted

`0.6.4-beta59` is deployed motion-disabled; the gate is DISARMED. Along the way
beta57 raised `max_linear_pulse_ceiling` 14 → 22 and rewrote the mid-drive
re-aim trigger, which un-accepted the execution profile — and **Gate 5 then
passed on it**, card-driven, 4/4 `target_reached` at 0.1038 / 0.0863 / 0.1261 /
0.1129 m (mean 0.1073) with every dispatched key byte-identical to
`LUBA_ACCEPTANCE_PROFILE`. `docs/gate5-beta57-PASSED-20260818.md`.

## 🚨 The headline finding: what shipped has never changed the mower's behaviour

Both halves of beta57 only differ from the old code in regimes neither hardware
run entered:

| change | differs only when | observed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| re-aim trigger | aim **15-19°** at range **≥ 0.5 m** | **0 of 15** decision points |
| ceiling 14 → 22 | leg needs **> 14 pulses** (~5 m) | 3 pulses used, of 22 |

Short legs cannot reach the trigger window, and the reason is geometric rather
than incidental: a segment opens with a turn, the post-turn gate holds the
residual under 10°, and aim error only grows as the arctangent of cross-track
over remaining range — so by the time aim reaches 15°, range has closed below
0.5 m. **Aim and range move in opposite directions**, and the trajectory skips
the window diagonally. Corpus replay puts all four old-vs-new divergences at
0.85-1.13 m range, 15.5-17.1° aim, on legs of **1.9-4.0 m**.

So: the profile is accepted on real evidence, and the control law is untested.
Those are different claims and this session kept them separate.

## `toward` is accurate to ~0.8°, and that reframes the night premise

169 forward pulses across 30 evidence files, from data already on disk:
`movement_heading + toward` = **89.819°**, median deviation **0.775°**, 98.2%
within 3°. Accuracy improves with travel length exactly as a fixed position-noise
floor predicts. **Exactly one outlier in 169** — and it is the already-recorded
item-15 case, a forward pulse immediately after a *night* turn.

⚠️ This corrects a framing, not a fact. `toward` is not too imprecise to steer
by; at ~1° it is comparable to VIO. What blocks closing a loop on it is **timing
and granularity** — bit-identical through a bounded pulse, arriving as one
post-hoc step, with a 48.15° ± 5.70 turn quantum that nothing scales.
`docs/toward-accuracy-measured-20260818.md`.

⚠️ Every sample is validated against **travel**, so this says nothing about
`toward` as a body heading after a rotation. That is item 15, still open, and the
sole outlier.

## Why the motion path depends on VIO at all

RTK answers *where am I and where is the target*; VIO answers **only** *which way
am I pointing*. Position, distance, progress, landing error and the length gates
are all RTK. Heading needs VIO because a single antenna cannot report facing at
rest and `location.RTK.yaw` is `None` on this hardware — no dual-antenna heading.
The dependency is a missing second antenna, not distrust of RTK.

## The five VIO fields, identified

All were shipped as HA sensors with full translations and none was documented.
`docs/vio-telemetry-fields.md`. Two things that were not written down anywhere:

- **`camera_brightness` maps its raw int oddly** — `0` and `1` are *flags*, any
  other value is a *level* with a threshold at 45. Raw `1` → Light, raw `40` →
  **Dark**. Not monotonic.
- **`vio_survival_distance` is how far the mower can navigate on vision alone**,
  identified from the APK: it appears only in the `fuseStatus == 2 || 3` branch,
  where the app swaps to a vision icon and shows a bar that goes orange below 50
  and red below 20. Our `0.0` is expected — this mower runs RTK Fix and has never
  entered a vision-fused state.

## 🚨 A measurable gap: detected features are never recorded

`_vio_feed` reads only `track_feature_num`, so `detect_feature_num` appears in
**zero samples** across the entire corpus. That distinction separates two
different faults:

- detected low → nothing to see (dark, blank grass)
- detected high, tracked low → sees plenty and **cannot match between frames**
  (motion blur, exposure hunting) — different cause, different remedy

Every VIO degradation on record is ambiguous between them.

## Process lessons worth keeping

- **A validation that passes by coincidence is worse than none.** The replay
  harness's first "4/4" was a false pass: it compared against a list mixing the
  post-turn gate with mid-drive re-aims, and omitted budget-blocked decisions —
  two errors that cancelled exactly.
- **Piping a gate through `tail` discards its verdict.** A pipeline's exit status
  is `tail`'s, so `check.py | tail -1 && git commit` commits through a failure.
  `pre-commit` is not installed as a git hook here either.
- **Claims about hardware go stale within a day.** The card's acceptance label
  was hardcoded on the 17th, Gate 5 passed on the 18th, and the card kept saying
  it owed a gate. Now pinned by a test that reads `docs/accepted-profile.json`.
- **Fixtures hide bugs that live state exposes.** The headless probe seeded
  `_validation = {valid: true}` and masked the empty-card blocker defect.

## New tools

| --- | --- |

# Part 2 — Roadmap

Ordered by information gained per unit of risk. Every hardware item is
daylight-only: the re-aim block sits inside `if turn_mode == "vio"` and requires
a live VIO track, and `vio_tracked_features` saturates at 80 so the feed looks
healthy right until it collapses.

## Phase 1 — Validate what is already shipped *(one daylight run)*

**Do the banked characterization run:** a leg of **≥ 1.9 m after a junction
turn**, junctions kept in the 45-70° band. This is the only thing that can make
the changed trigger fire.

Watch: whether any mid-drive correction fires at all — none ever has on the new
code — and if one does, whether the budget holds. ⚠️ A leg after a junction turn
has an effective mid-drive budget of **2, not 3**: the post-turn gate spends the
same counter.

Then run `scripts/replay_reaim_trigger.py` on the evidence; it will say directly
whether old and new diverged.

**Done when:** at least one mid-drive correction has fired on the new trigger,
and we know whether 3 corrections suffice. Until then beta57 is shipped,
accepted, and unproven.

## Phase 2 — Close the instrumentation gap *(no mower needed)*

Add `detect_feature_num` to `_vio_feed`. One line, no runtime cost, and it makes
every future VIO degradation diagnosable instead of ambiguous. Deliberately not
done as a drive-by because it changes what the executor records on every run —
it belongs with the next change touching that function, which is this.

While there: consider recording the heading *source* per pulse, so a later
session can tell a VIO-derived facing from a travel-derived one without
reconstructing it.

## Phase 3 — Reach the original goal: 50 ft on one click

The ask that started this was **50 ft (15.24 m) from a single waypoint**. The
route taken (raise the per-segment cap) got to an authorized 6.10 m and is
unvalidated above 4 m. **Route B remains the cheaper path and was never
refuted:** auto-split one long click into ≤ 4 collinear sub-legs of ≤ 3.81 m.

Why it is attractive: every sub-leg sits *inside* the measured 4 m envelope,
collinear junctions are ~0° turns so they add no turn translation and no
post-turn residual, and each sub-leg gets a fresh correction budget. It changes
no profile key, so it owes no Gate 5.

Cost: it consumes the whole per-click segment budget
(`REAL_CLICK_TO_GO_SEGMENT_LIMIT = 4`), so a 50 ft click cannot also carry other
waypoints.

Sequence: Phase 1 first — if the trigger turns out to need work, that lands
before splitting legs on top of it.

## Phase 4 — Reliability, which is the operator's actual goal

*"Click-to-go reliable enough to trust without watching."* What stands between
here and there, in order:

1. ✅ **The motion gate keeps being left armed — BUILT 2026-08-18.** Found ON at
   rest **three times** in one day, once with zero blockers and the mower off its
   dock, with no docked-position backstop.

   ⚠️ **A pure-YAML automation was impossible**: the gate is a config entry
   *option*, so there was no entity to toggle and no service to call — only a
   `binary_sensor` reporting the state. It needed
   `mammotion.disarm_experimental_motion`, which now exists.

   🔒 **The service is ONE-WAY and there is deliberately no arm counterpart.**
   Arming stays behind the options flow, where a human does it in Settings; a
   service that could arm would let any automation, script, scene or voice
   assistant open the motion gate — a strictly larger attack surface than exists
   today. This one can only ever close it, so the worst a bug or stray call can
   do is refuse to move. It also refuses while a session is active, so it can
   never half-stop a moving mower; that is Abort/Stop, a different path.

   Automation: `docs/automations/disarm-motion-gate.yaml` — fires 15 minutes
   after the gate reads ready-and-idle, plus a nightly sweep, and notifies only
   when it actually changed something so the alert does not become noise.
   ✅ **Installed on the host 2026-08-22** as
   `automation.mammotion_disarm_motion_gate_when_left_armed` (id
   `1755900000001`), appended to `/config/automations.yaml` and loaded by
   `automation.reload`. It did not need a release: the YAML is host config, not
   integration code.
2. **A failure taxonomy from the run history.** The card already stores 10 runs.
   Every `stop_reason` the executor can emit should have a one-line meaning and a
   "what to do", the way blocker codes now do. Turning 20+ armed runs into a
   table of *how* runs end is the cheapest reliability data available and needs no
   mower.
3. **BLE remains a hint, not a number** — ~7% of runs aborting, 95% CI 0.2-31.9%.
   ⚠️ Do not fund BLE work on that. If reliability becomes the priority, first
   *measure* it: the population is the run history, not intuition.

## Phase 5 — The capability jump: named destinations

The natural product of click-to-go is not more clicking. It is **saved, named
destinations** — "send the mower to the shed" as an HA service or button, usable
from an automation or a voice assistant.

Everything needed already exists: validated paths, a bounded executor, safety
gates, and now a mechanically-verified profile. What is missing is persistence
and a trigger surface. This is the step that turns a supervised diagnostic tool
into something the household uses, and it depends on Phase 4 rather than Phase 1
— you would not automate a path you still have to watch.

## Explicitly not proposed

- **Night work.** Parked by standing decision. ⚠️ Note only that the ~0.8°
  `toward` result changes the *premise* the parking was reasoned on — the barrier
  is latency and quantum, not accuracy. That is the operator's call to reopen or
  not, and this document does not ask.
- **Accuracy work.** Closed by standing decision at ~0.089 m mean against a
  0.15 m tolerance; the 0.065 m intercept is a sensing floor, not a tuning target.
- **Upstream-shaped work** — auto-derivation, per-device calibration. This yard
  only.
- **The iPhone card lockup** is open but unrelated to motion; it is logged in
  `docs/NEXT-SESSION.md` with two hypotheses already refuted and a capture
  checklist. It should not be allowed to delay or contaminate a hardware run.
